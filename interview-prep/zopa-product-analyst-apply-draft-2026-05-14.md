## Open-ended questions

### Q1: Why Zopa?

> Zopa is the cleanest match I've found for the pivot I'm making — from two-time founder to Product Analyst inside a UK fintech where SQL, experiment design, and cross-functional partnership across Product, Engineering and the wider business is the daily job. I'm most drawn to the **Mobile App tribe**: owning the app as a horizontal platform and being accountable for in-app conversion and product adoption is the exact intersection of my last three years — I shipped a React Native app (Sfocra) and a full-stack AI assistant (Molebie AI, 28 days) where I had to own both the experience surface and the acquisition metric end-to-end. **CRM** is a strong second — moving from campaign-led to customer-led across 1.7M customers is the kind of early-stage analytics build where founder reps (cross-functional ownership, scrappy infrastructure, defining the metric before the experiment) actually compound. As a UK customer who's watched Zopa scale from peer-lending into a full bank, I want to be on the side of that growth that designs the experiments, not just reads the dashboard.

### Q2: Tell us about a relevant project or proof point.

> **Molebie AI (open-source full-stack AI assistant, Mar 2026 → present).** I architected and shipped a full-stack AI assistant in 28 days — Python/FastAPI backend, React frontend, SQLite persistence layer, and a hybrid RAG document memory where I wrote both the schema and the SQL queries that power retrieval. The piece that's relevant for the Mobile App / CRM tribe: I built a modular inference layer that swaps across 5 LLM backends (MLX, Ollama, vLLM, llama.cpp, OpenAI) behind one API, so I could side-by-side compare providers on real prompts in under a minute. That swap surface is, in effect, an in-product experimentation harness — I designed the comparison, picked defaults per prompt class based on the readout, and used the discipline to break down an ambiguous "which model is best" question into clear analytical approaches per prompt class. The project was selected for Y Combinator's AI Startup School Stockholm 2026 (~1 of 1,350 from 4,000 applicants) and drew inbound investor interest from 42CAP (Munich). **Reflection:** the v1 of that swap surface is essentially "support the design and analysis of experiments to test product changes" — Zopa's language, almost verbatim. The lesson is that the metric and the variant have to be defined before the experiment starts, not after; I'd carry that discipline into the tribe from day one.
