In a regulated, clinical-trial domain that reliability problem isn't a nice-to-have, it's the whole product, and it's the part of AI engineering I find most interesting. ### Q2: Tell us about a relevant project or proof point.
> I built Molebie AI — an open-source full-stack AI assistant — solo in 28 days (Python/FastAPI backend, React/TypeScript frontend, SQLite/RAG store: the same stack Pharosyn runs). The hard part was reliability: I engineered a modular inference layer across 5 LLM backends and a hybrid RAG pipeline (vector + BM25 + reranking) with hallucination-verification (CoVe, SelfCheckGPT) so answers could be trusted, plus a CLI onboarding wizard that cut setup from hours to under 5 minutes. It's not a weekend hack — 238 commits, a CI pipeline with CodeQL + secret-scanning + pip-audit, Docker, MIT-licensed (github.com/Jimmy6929/Molebie_AI). It was selected for YC AI Startup School Stockholm 2026 and drew inbound interest from 42CAP. What I took from it: shipping fast and shipping *trustworthy* aren't opposites if you build the verification in from day one — which is the muscle Pharosyn needs in pharma.
